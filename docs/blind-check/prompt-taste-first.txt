You are a senior paid social marketer. A client sells wool running sneakers at $120 (a sustainable brand, Allbirds is the closest competitor) and wants to launch on Meta in London. You have a $3,000 test budget for 4 weeks. Two strategists sent the plans below. Do not guess how they were made; judge them only as plans.

PLAN 1
Audience: skews toward women, most of all ages 30 to 34, less so 45 and over. Design minded, fitness casual and sustainability aware: they share taste with minimalist running and lifestyle brands, studio fitness platforms, folk and soul music, and wellness and nature TV.
Interests to target: Tracksmith; Outdoor Voices; Everlane; ClassPass; Peloton; Nike Training Club; Warby Parker; Maggie Rogers.
Tests:
  1. Fans of minimalist running labels respond to a clean, design led creative more than to a generic eco message. Target Tracksmith, Outdoor Voices and Nike Training Club, women 25 to 40 in London. Minimal flat lay, hook "Run in wool. Look like you didn't try." Watch CTR. Stop if CTR is under the control ad set after 3,000 impressions.
  2. Transparency minded shoppers respond to proof. Target Everlane and ClassPass, women 28 to 40 in London. Material breakdown, hook "$120. Here is exactly what is in them." Watch cost per add to cart. Stop if it is 30% above the control after 50 clicks.
  3. Folk and soul fans respond to a mood led, easy run video. Target Maggie Rogers and Leon Bridges fans, 25 to 40, London. 15 second easy run video with a folk soundtrack. Watch 3 second view rate. Stop if it is under the control after 3,000 impressions.
Partners: Tracksmith (co-host a London community shakeout run); ClassPass (trial pair or code for members via a studio run club); a British soul artist soundtracking a short run film; a host read ad on a wellness podcast.

PLAN 2
Audience: urban professionals in London aged 25 to 40 who run casually or train for local races, care about sustainability, and will pay a mid premium price for comfortable, good looking shoes. They know Allbirds and want a more performance minded alternative that still feels low impact.
Interests to target: Allbirds; Running; Sustainable fashion; Parkrun; Strava; Veja; London Marathon.
Tests:
  1. Allbirds fans respond best to a comfort and sustainability message. Target Allbirds and sustainable fashion, ages 25 to 40, London, with a "Soft on you, light on the planet" creative. Watch cost per purchase and CTR. Stop if cost per purchase is above 1.5 times target after 1,000 clicks.
  2. Runners care more about performance than eco claims. Target running, Strava and Parkrun with a "From desk to Hampstead Heath" creative. Watch add to cart rate. Stop if add to cart rate is below 3 percent after 7 days.
  3. Transparent footprint data builds trust. Retarget site visitors and video viewers with a "Proof, not slogans" carbon footprint creative. Watch conversion rate and ROAS. Stop if ROAS stays below 1.5 after 10 days.
Partners: local London run clubs (demo runs at weekly meetups); Parkrun communities (post run coffee with trial pairs); micro influencers in sustainable living and running; a shoe recycling take back scheme.

Answer briefly:
1. Which plan would you fund first, and why (3 sentences at most)?
2. Score each plan from 1 to 10 on: fit with this product and buyer; how specific and ready to run it is; how different it is from what every competitor already targets; risk of wasting the budget (10 = lowest risk).
3. Which 3 individual tests from either plan would you actually run first? Name them by plan and number.
4. What would you check before spending a dollar on the plan you picked?
