A decision Qloo changed

The goal is not to predict a customer. It is to help a performance marketer select a more defensible A/B test before spending money on ads.

The case: $120 wool running sneakers

Seed: Allbirds, a comparable brand; the shoe itself is not known to Qloo. Primary market: London. Comparison: Hanoi.

Without Qloo

Ungrounded plain-LLM suggestions in the owner's demonstration included runner-oriented interests such as Strava, parkrun and athletic shoe competitors.

With Qloo

Owner-reported live results included studio-fitness and wellness affinities: ClassPass, Peloton, Outdoor Voices and Tracksmith.

Decision: test a studio-fitness/wellness creative hypothesis against the original performance-running concept. Do not discard the runner hypothesis merely because it did not appear in one Qloo result set.

Precision: Qloo's seed demographic indexes of women +0.33 and ages 30–34 +0.42 are aggregate indexes for the seed entity, not percentages, not London resident demographics, and not evidence of purchases. The example above is user-reported from a 20 Oct live run; this static page does not independently authenticate that run.

Reproduce using the actual live demo

  1. Open the Taste Brief demo; enter the private judge access code from the Devpost submission.
  2. Choose the example Wool sneakers in London and Hanoi, or enter wool running sneakers, $120; London, UK; Hanoi, Vietnam; Allbirds.
  3. Confirm the result banner says Live run on real Qloo data. If it says synthetic, it is not evidence of live integration.
  4. Inspect the Qloo call trace and entity citations for each line. Check the actual returned names and affinities, not just the count of cited lines.
  5. Review What to test first, the comparative top-12 rankings and the no-data baseline. Live outputs may vary by Qloo ranking and generation.

What the evidence does not establish

  • Source-backed is not factually proven: the code checks Qloo-returned entity IDs, cited affinity numbers and certain item names, not whether a marketer's causal interpretation is correct.
  • Similarity is not demand: Qloo aggregate taste affinities cannot prove who will purchase a product.
  • City signal is not a city survey: non-place results are location-adjusted ranked lists; “not in the top 12” never means “unpopular in that city”.
  • Meta compatibility is unknown: a Qloo entity name may not exist as a selectable Meta Ads interest.
  • No impact measurement yet: this is a testable campaign hypothesis, not demonstrated incremental sales or ROAS.
