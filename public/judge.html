Qloo picks the podcasts brands really sponsor

Taste Brief tells a brand which podcasts to sponsor, which interests to target and which ad to test first, each pick with the Qloo result behind it. The headline test is graded by where brands really sponsor, not by Qloo.

The test: which podcasts do brands really sponsor?

Sponsor reads in the shows' own episode notes (public RSS) are the answer key; Qloo never sees them. For each brand, Qloo ranks the shows by how much the brand's audience likes them, and we check how often a show the brand sponsors ranks above one it doesn't (AUC; 0.50 is a coin flip). A show counts as sponsored when it reads the brand in at least 2 episodes of the past year.

Locked first: hypotheses, measures, baselines and pass rules were fingerprinted with SHA-256 and pushed to the public repo before any held-out show was read (PREREGISTRATION.md, commit c4d6732), and the analysis code before any score was fetched. The pilot shows (Apple's US top 200) are not in the test, which uses Apple's US charts in 12 genres. Check the fingerprints on any OS with python scripts/podcast/verify_lock.py.

Pre-registered hypotheses: all pass on both labels

  • H1, Qloo beats chance: above 0.5 for 51 of 62 brands (65 of 76).
  • H2, Qloo beats the chart and popularity: +9 to +16 points over the chart, +8 to +16 over popularity (95% intervals; +10 to +17 and +10 to +19 pre-registered).
  • H3, Qloo adds to ad volume: the two ranks combined beat ad volume alone by +4 to +10 points (+5 to +11).
  • H4, exploratory: by how widely Qloo's scores spread across shows: 0.66 against 0.64 (0.70 against 0.62 pre-registered).

Exploratory checks, not pre-registered

  • Specific to the brand: ranked by other brands' Qloo scores, the same shows score 0.57; by the brand's own, 0.65. Ahead for 47 of 61 brands, +4 to +13 points. About half of the lift over chance is specific to the brand; the rest is shows advertisers like in general.
  • Within genre: only shows in the same genre compared: Qloo 0.62, chart 0.55. Ahead of the chart in all 12 genres; weakest in leisure (below chance) and sports.
  • Recent deals: 43 sponsorships that appear only in a show's 10 newest episodes: Qloo 0.62 (0.51 to 0.72), chart 0.45; established deals 0.65. Suggestive only; the prospective check above is the real test.
  • Where it adds least: for the 36 brands with 10 or more sponsored shows, a genre-based prior does almost as well (0.61 against 0.63).
  • By brand (per-brand AUC on 12 to 22 shows, so noisy): it works for brands bought on audience fit, such as Ritual 0.82, Skims 0.79, Honeylove 0.77, each above its genre prior and other brands' scores; it fails for meal kits and mass-reach buyers (Factor 0.42, Zocdoc 0.45, Casper 0.37).
  • Competitor pairs fixed in advance: 4 of the 6 could run (ExpressVPN and Helix Sleep are not in Qloo). In betting a competitor's audience predicts almost as well (DraftKings from FanDuel 0.70 against 0.72), but sports genre alone does too; the other pairs are mixed. Descriptive only.
  • The other direction does not work: for a show looking for sponsors, Qloo ranks the brands that sponsor it at 0.62, while pitching the biggest spenders does better (0.74). The app does not offer that mode. reverse.json

Labels: 43 of 100 random matches of the pre-registered dictionary were sponsor reads, so a stricter label (whole word plus a sponsor cue nearby) was declared in DEVIATION-1.md before any analysis: 82 of 100. Reproduce it

  1. Fastest: open the demo and press See a real run now. It shows the last real run of the example instantly, with when it was recorded.
  2. For a fresh run, use the judge link from the submission (it carries the access code), or one of today's free live runs.
  3. Click the example Wool sneakers in London and Hanoi and press Build the brief. A live run takes one to two minutes; every step shows up as it happens.
  4. The strip above the result says Live run on real Qloo data. Sample data is labelled as such.
  5. Qloo rankings and the model's wording vary a little between runs.

What the checks prove, and what they don't

  • Sponsorships show where brands spend, not that the spend paid off. No ads have been run by us; the tests in the app are how a marketer finds out. The sponsor labels were checked by an AI, not a person.
  • Citations for anything other than the seed brand are checked against Qloo results from the same run, with matching names and numbers. The code does not verify the full meaning of each claim.
  • For brands, artists and TV the city only nudges Qloo's ranking; places are filtered to each city. Qloo's podcasts are mostly US shows: with London as the location signal the order changes but the shows stay American, so the page ranks on the brand's audience and flags this. Interest names still need checking in Meta Ads Manager.
