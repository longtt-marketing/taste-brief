The test: which podcasts do brands sponsor?

Sponsor reads in the shows' own episode notes (public RSS, 2,124 Apple US chart shows in 12 genres, up to 40 episodes from the past year) are the answer key; Qloo never sees them. For each brand, Qloo ranks the shows by how much the brand's audience likes them, and we check how often a show the brand sponsors ranks above one it doesn't (AUC; 0.50 is a coin flip). A show counts as sponsored when it reads the brand in at least 2 episodes of the past year.

Locked first: hypotheses, measures, baselines and pass rules were fingerprinted with SHA-256 and pushed to the public repo before any held-out show was read (PREREGISTRATION.md, commit c4d6732), and the analysis code before any score was fetched. The pilot shows (Apple's US top 200) are not in the test, which uses Apple's US charts in 12 genres. Check the fingerprints on any OS with python scripts/podcast/verify_lock.py.

Pre-registered hypotheses: all pass on both labels

  • H1, Qloo beats chance: above 0.5 for 51 of 62 brands (65 of 76).
  • H2, Qloo beats the chart and popularity: +9 to +16 points over the chart, +8 to +16 over popularity (95% intervals; +10 to +17 and +10 to +19 pre-registered).
  • H3, Qloo adds to ad volume: the two ranks combined beat ad volume alone by +4 to +10 points (+5 to +11).
  • H4, exploratory: by how widely Qloo's scores spread across shows: 0.66 against 0.64 (0.70 against 0.62 pre-registered).

Exploratory checks, not pre-registered

  • Labels a script can confirm: 84% of strict sponsorships (1,211 of 1,446) also carry the brand's own web address in the same episode notes (drinklmnt.com/liz and the like). Counting only those, Qloo scores 0.64 (0.60 to 0.69) against the chart's 0.52, 53 brands, 41 above chance. confirmed.json
  • A person, blind: the entrant read the same 100 strict samples on a page that showed only the show, the brand and the snippet, never the AI's labels, though the 82 of 100 total was already published: 82 sponsor reads, 15 not, 3 unsure. That agrees with Claude on all 97 the reader was sure of; Claude had marked the 3 unsure ones as not sponsor reads. The entrant built the project, so a buyer outside it would be a stronger check. ChatGPT, from the snippets alone, also agreed on all 100, though it had seen the 82 of 100 total earlier in the conversation. precision-check-strict-human.json, precision-check-strict-rater2.json
  • Specific to the brand: ranked by other brands' Qloo scores, the same shows score 0.57; by the brand's own, 0.65. Ahead for 47 of 61 brands, +4 to +13 points. About half of the lift over chance is specific to the brand; the rest is shows advertisers like in general.
  • Within genre: only shows in the same genre compared: Qloo 0.62, chart 0.55. Ahead of the chart in all 12 genres on these labels; on the pre-registered labels in 11, with true crime level (0.569 against 0.572). Weakest in leisure (below chance) and sports.
  • Recent deals: 43 sponsorships that appear only in a show's 10 newest episodes: Qloo 0.62 (0.51 to 0.72), chart 0.45; established deals 0.65. Suggestive only; the prospective check above is the real test.
  • Where it adds least: for the 36 brands with 10 or more sponsored shows, a genre-based prior does almost as well (0.61 against 0.63).
  • By brand (per-brand AUC on 12 to 22 shows, so noisy): it works for brands bought on audience fit, such as Ritual 0.82, Skims 0.79, Honeylove 0.77, each above its genre prior and other brands' scores; it fails for meal kits and mass-reach buyers (Factor 0.42, Zocdoc 0.45, Casper 0.37).
  • Competitor pairs fixed in advance: 4 of the 6 could run (ExpressVPN and Helix Sleep are not in Qloo). In betting, a competitor's audience predicts almost as well (DraftKings from FanDuel 0.70 against 0.72), but sports genre alone does too; the other pairs are mixed. Descriptive only.
  • The other direction does not work: for a show looking for sponsors, Qloo ranks the brands that sponsor it at 0.62, while pitching the biggest spenders does better (0.74). The app does not offer that mode. reverse.json

The agent checks its own picks before you see them

When the agent submits its brief, the app scores each Meta interest with Qloo on the audience of a direct rival the agent names but never used (up to three names, tried in order; never a seed or a cited result; if Qloo knows none of them, the closest uncited brand from the run, and the page says so). Strong is 0.9 or more, fair 0.8 to 0.9. A pick below 0.8, or one Qloo cannot score for that audience, goes back to the agent once with the scores, to replace with a result it already has or to drop; the rewritten brief is checked again and both rounds are shown on the page and in the interests CSV. Since 22 Oct the same check also looks each interest up in Meta's interest search (one batched request), and an interest Meta does not have goes back the same way, with Meta's nearest names when it has any, and the instruction that no targeting test may name it.

What the rewrites change. On 10 benchmark products (cases 3 to 12 of the benchmark below), one live run each: the agent rewrote 5 briefs. Each first and final brief was graded on the benchmark's held-out competitor, a brand fixed on 21 Oct that the agent never saw. In the 7 cases where that brand is not the agent's own rival, three briefs were rewritten. Together they dropped six picks Qloo could not score for that audience and added three: one at 0.9 or more (Casamigos, for a hard seltzer), one fair (Care/of, 0.82) and one with no exact match in Qloo (Clinique). The share of picks at 0.9 or more went from 74% to 80%, mostly because unscorable picks left. So the check mainly removes interests nobody can verify; it rarely finds better ones. The agent gets one rewrite, and the second check is shown as it comes out. Exploratory, 10 runs. docs/selfcheck-eval.json, scripts/selfcheck-eval.mjs.

What the Meta check changes. Plan locked before any run (docs/meta-loop/PLAN.md): the same 10 benchmark products plus the page's example, one live run each, first brief against final brief. Targeting tests that name an interest Meta does not have: 14 in the first briefs, 3 in the final ones. Share of interests that exist in Meta as named: 54% to 89%. The guard, picks at 0.9 or more on the benchmark's held-out competitor (8 runs where that brand is not the agent's rival): 80% to 76%, inside the 10-point cost the plan allowed. The plan's bar was at most 1 run of 11 with such a test left; 3 runs had one, so the bar is missed. Since then a gate in plain code (readyTests in public/verdict.js, tested) keeps any test that names a missing interest out of the locked plan and marks it "not ready to launch", so no such test is ever locked as ready, whatever the model writes. The agent gets one rewrite. docs/meta-loop/results.json, scripts/meta-loop-eval.mjs.

The outreach drafts are checked in code too, on 10 products (the first is the page's own product, in Chicago): does the email body ask for rate, downloads, host-read slots, dates and category exclusivity, carry the proposed code and open with the show's name; does any draft hold a number or a listed product claim the input did not give; does the host read say a number twice, pad, or run past 80 words. Since 22 Oct each draft is written from that show's own description and tags. The latest run passed 17 of 18; the one flag is the checker reading "40" in E-40, a rapper's name Qloo returned, not an invented number. Drafts for different shows of one product (the 6 products with two or more drafts) now share 60% of their email words and 31% of their host-read words once the show's name and code are taken out (word overlap, mean over pairs), down from 96% and 82% before: they are no longer one template with the name swapped. Earlier runs: 11 of 18 on the first, mostly false alarms in the checker, then 18 of 18 twice. One case matches the wrong Qloo brand (Money for Monarch Money), so its drafts name the wrong reference brand; the file reports the matched name for every case. A pass is a code check, not a person's review. docs/outreach-eval.json, scripts/outreach-eval.mjs.

Does the agent choose better shows than a fixed rule?

No measurable difference. The plan was pushed before the run (docs/agent-vs-rule/PLAN.md, with one logged change before any result: the model does not accept a forced tool call). Agent minus rule: +0.02 hits per brand (95% interval −0.04 to +0.08; agent ahead for 2 brands, rule for 1, tied for 45). They chose the same three shows for 24 of the 48. Taste fit and popularity alone score a little higher on this measure, because the agent and the rule both lean toward shows where no rival is found yet, while brands often buy where rivals already are (the open-shows check above is the other side of this). The agent also picks shows we have no ad data for 22 times, which can never count as a hit. 10 brands had no show in the ad data among their 20 and 4 could not be run (3 times the model did not pick, once Qloo had no data). When that happens in the app, the page's fixed rule picks the shows and the page says so (Chosen by the fixed rule). So the agent's value is not a better ranking: it gives a reason for each pick and weighs whether the show's subject suits the product. docs/agent-vs-rule/results.json, scripts/agent-vs-rule.mjs.

What the checks prove, and what they don't

  • Sponsorships show where brands spend, not that the spend paid off. We ran no ads; the tests in the app, and a promo code per show, are how a marketer finds out. A script labels the sponsorships (a brand name with a sponsor cue nearby); Claude and a person each checked the same random 100 of its strict matches in the held-out shows (the same 82 sponsor reads). 84% also carry the brand's own web address in the notes, which proves a promotion but not a paid contract.
  • Citations for anything other than the seed brand are checked against Qloo results from the same run, with matching names and numbers. The code does not verify the full meaning of each claim.
  • For brands, artists and TV the city only nudges Qloo's ranking; places are filtered to each city. Qloo's podcasts are mostly US shows: with London as the location signal the order changes but the shows stay American, so the page ranks on the brand's audience and flags this. Every interest is looked up in Meta's interest search inside the self-check, and a missing one goes back to the agent once; Meta has dropped many niche brands, so a strong Qloo fit can still be missing there. A targeting test that still names a missing interest after the rewrite is marked not ready to launch.
